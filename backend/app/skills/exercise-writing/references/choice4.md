# choice4: four options, one gap

Fields: `stem` with exactly one `___`, four distinct `options`, `other_fillings`, and
`correct` (one of the options, written exactly the same). In `other_fillings`, before
you write `correct`, go through the wrong options and anything else a learner might
put in the gap, and say what in the sentence rules each one out. If you cannot say it
for one of them, change the sentence.

- The wrong options are mistakes a learner without the rule really makes (see the
  grammar point's common errors), and each one is clearly wrong in this sentence - not
  just less natural. Exactly one option fits.
- Keep the options parallel: same length and shape, differing only where the rule
  decides. An option that is odd for another reason (a misspelling nobody makes, a
  different meaning) gives the answer away.
- Before fixing `correct`, try each wrong option in the sentence. If one could be right
  in some reading, change the context.
- For the passive, no wrong option may make a right sentence of another kind: an
  adjective ("are clean", "is closed") or a verb that works without a doer ("broke",
  "opened", "sold"). Use a verb that needs one (steal, build, invite, write) or a "by"
  phrase, and make the wrong options form mistakes ("are cleaning", "cleaned", "is
  cleaned").

Good (modal perfect):
- stem: "I feel sick now. I ___ so much cake at the party."
- options: "shouldn't have eaten", "shouldn't have ate", "shouldn't had eaten",
  "mustn't eat"
- correct: "shouldn't have eaten"
- Why it works: "now ... at the party" makes it a past regret; each wrong option is a
  real form error or the present.

Bad:
- stem: "I ___ so much cake."
- options: "shouldn't have eaten", "didn't eat", "ate", "shouldn't eat"
- Why it fails: without context "didn't eat" and "ate" are also fine sentences, and
  "shouldn't eat" is fine as advice. Nothing in the sentence asks for a regret.

Bad (passive):
- stem: "These rooms ___ every day, so they always look nice."
- options: "cleaned", "is cleaned", "are cleaned", "are clean"
- Why it fails: "These rooms are clean every day" is also right.
  Better: "These rooms ___ by the hotel staff every morning.", options "are cleaned",
  "are cleaning", "cleaned", "is cleaned".
